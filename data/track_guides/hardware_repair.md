# Hardware Repair

## Overview
Hardware repair diagnoses and resolves problems involving computers, peripherals, and related components. The work combines observation, safe handling, careful testing, and accurate records. A technician should distinguish a hardware fault from an operating-system, configuration, or connectivity issue before replacing parts. Entry-level experience is built through supervised practice and controlled exercises that prioritize equipment and personal safety.

## Core skills
Computer components; diagnostic reasoning; electrostatic-discharge awareness; safe tool handling; peripheral troubleshooting; operating-system installation basics; device documentation; customer communication; parts identification.

## Typical entry-level roles
Computer repair technician, bench technician, desktop support technician, and device refurbishment assistant are common role titles. Responsibilities depend on the equipment and service environment.

## What each stage means for this track
### Foundations
Learn component names, device connections, power basics, and safe handling. Understand how to identify a system and record its symptoms.
### Build Skills
Practice inspections and simple diagnostics on equipment you are authorized to service. Follow a repeatable process and isolate one possible fault at a time.
### Prove Skills
Document a supervised repair or diagnostic exercise, including symptoms, tests, findings, and verification after the work.
### Apply
Show careful records, safety awareness, and examples of troubleshooting. Review role duties and apply when training and experience match.

## Common beginner mistakes
Replacing a component before confirming the fault can waste time or create new problems. Avoid opening equipment without authorization, ignoring electrostatic-discharge precautions, or forcing connectors and fasteners. Do not assume a device is permanently damaged before checking power, cables, settings, and symptoms. Follow supervision and safety procedures.
